# Collectors: analysis log (moved from 01-collectors.md)

A dated log in the order it was written. Each statement was true on its date, so check counts and limits against the scripts and `docs/agents/studies.md` before relying on them. Current tools: `analyze-collectors.mjs`, `analyze-episodes.mjs`.

## Offline analysis (analyze-collectors.mjs, 2026-10-24)
The monthly judgement ("coverage, variance, and whether a collector leads the next 5 minutes of volatility") has a first tool: `analyze-collectors.mjs` at the repo root. It is not part of the app (no release). Coverage, lead-in and the constant-field check are exact; the lead test is a Spearman rank correlation between a feature taken at a record's time and the realised volatility (and absolute move) of the Binance mark over the following horizon (default 5 minutes), built from the 10-second `basis` marks. It is descriptive only. Real conclusions need weeks of data, several regimes, and a better test than overlapping windows (blocked or subsampled windows, partial correlations against current volatility, so that a feature is not credited just for echoing the volatility we already have). Those are the next steps when there is data to look at.
Self-checks: `node analyze-collectors.mjs --selftest` builds a synthetic tape (a volatility index that leads by construction, a noise field, a constant field, a gap) and checks the numbers.

## Better evaluation (analyze-collectors.mjs, 2026-10-24)
Replaces the first lead test (a plain rank correlation, which credited any feature that merely echoed the volatility we already have and had no real significance: 21 features and overlapping windows). The plan is in the script (`PLAN`, `node analyze-collectors.mjs --plan`) and was fixed before any real data: target = realised volatility of the Binance mark over the next 5 minutes (the absolute move is built but not judged); control = realised volatility over the previous 5 minutes; statistic = partial rank correlation; inference = circular-shift permutation (999 shifts, keeps the autocorrelation); a feature passes the first half at p <= 0.05 / 21 and |rho| >= 0.08, and is CONFIRMED only if the second half has the same sign, p <= 0.05 and |rho| >= 0.04; at least 300 samples a half (one sample per feature per 10 seconds). `--merge` joins tapes and sessions, counting identical records once.
Self-test (16 checks, `--selftest`): a feature that knows the next regime is CONFIRMED; a copy of the previous volatility has a raw correlation of about 0.27 but a partial one near zero and is not confirmed; noise and a constant are not; features without records say not enough data; the verdict rules; the same seed gives the same answer.
Known limits, written down on purpose:
- A feature that knows the present sooner than five minutes of price history (the volatility index, implied vol, a book that reacts at once) will register as predictive, because the control lags. That is useful information, but it is not a forecast. A shorter second control (the last two minutes) would narrow it, at the cost of noise.
- One sample per feature per 10 seconds, ranks and a single horizon (5 minutes): other horizons, other targets (direction, the Reader's episodes) are not judged.
- It judges volatility, not the Reader's families. The more useful second analysis is episode-conditioned (what did each collector read when an episode was detected, against your agree / disagree labels); it needs recordings with labels and the engine's replay, and is not built.
- With a few days of data nothing will reach the corrected threshold unless the effect is large; "not enough data" and "nothing" are the honest answers for weeks.

## Episode analysis (analyze-episodes.mjs, 2026-10-24)
The second analysis promised above: judged against what the Reader detects and what you say about it, not against volatility. `node analyze-episodes.mjs SESSION... [--labels reader_labels.json] [--tape tape_*.json] [--cfg cfg.json] [--all] [--json]`.
- **Unit:** the episode group (`episodeGroups()`), one per event, as labels attach to groups. Detection time = `tDetected`. For each feature the newest collector record at or before it, within 3x its usual interval (at least 2 minutes; 15 minutes for the 5-minute ratios), is turned into a percentile of that feature's values in all the data given (one value per 10 s per feature; at least 50 values).
- **Labels:** matched with the app's own rule (family, venue, side, overlapping times; a `missed` label never labels a group; the newest matching label wins).
- **Output per family:** overview and outcome counts; context (median percentile far from 50); agree vs disagree (difference of mean percentiles, permutation p, `suggestive` below 0.05 / 21); the two commonest outcomes against each other. Needs 6 groups per side before a contrast prints.
- **Self-test (8 checks):** 14 simulated bursts with collector data that differs between the agreed and disagreed ones (found, suggestive; the noise feature is not), the labels matched as the app does, percentile and contrast on their own, and the label rule. A first version used the difference of medians and was too coarse for 7 against 7 (a perfect separation gave p = 0.02): it is the mean now.
- **Limits:** thresholds are the engine defaults unless `--cfg`; a session needs `ext` and the Reader inputs; groups in one session are not independent (an event cluster), and the p-values do not account for that, so read a suggestive line as a place to look; it cannot say whether a feature would have helped detect an event, only whether it differed when one was found; nothing here has run on real data yet.

## Correction after the first real data (2026-10-24, ~23:30 auto-recording and 2.7 hours of marks)
Run on the real tape (14,196 records) and the first auto-recording (16 minutes, 27 groups), the first version of the evaluation said `bnDerivs.oi` was CONFIRMED on 2.7 hours of one calm evening, and the episode context put the funding rate at the 80th to 90th percentile for nearly every group. Both were artefacts, found by reading the output instead of believing it:
- **Drifting levels:** open interest (and funding, and the volatility index) slide slowly all night, volatility also decays through a calm night, and a circular-shift permutation test cannot see a shared trend. Fix: every feature enters as its deviation from its own trailing 30-minute mean (`PLAN.detrendSec`), and the self-test's synthetic night now has a decaying volatility plus an open interest sliding down: the old plan gave that open interest rho +0.34 (p 0.001) in the first half and nearly confirmed it (second half p 0.19); the new plan gives rho +0.15 (p 0.13), `nothing`.
- **Rehearsal label:** under 24 hours of marks or fewer than 2 calendar days (`PLAN.minHours`, `minDays`) every CONFIRMED or promising verdict now reads `rehearsal only (...)`.
- **Episode context:** the percentile is now of the deviation from the feature's own 30-minute mean, both for the value at detection and for the reference, so a slow level no longer puts a whole recording at one extreme. Within a single recording the slow context is shared by every group anyway: contrasts need many sessions in different regimes.
